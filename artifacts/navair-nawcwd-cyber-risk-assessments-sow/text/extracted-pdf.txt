1.0 Background
Recent global cyber events reinforce that cyber threats to U.S. infrastructure and military
systems are real and significant. Cyberspace is traditionally defined as "a global domain within
the information environment consisting of the interdependent network of information technology
infrastructures, including the internet, telecommunications networks, computer systems and
embedded processors and controllers” (Joint Publication 3-12 (R) Cyberspace Operations). The
focus of this Statement of Work (SOW) is embedded processors and controllers, and Real Time
Operating Systems (RTOS).

We live and operate in a wired world and rely on communications technologies; from computers,
data centers and the cloud to mobile connectivity. Economies rely on this wired world to operate
as does the military. DoD systems must be sufficiently robust, resilient and sustainable to operate
in the increasingly contested and connected cyberspace domain. To know a systems
effectiveness, robustness and resiliency, analyses must be performed to determine and mitigate
system weaknesses. Per the FY16 NDAA section 1647 DoD is directed to perform Cyber Risk
Assessments (CRAs) on all major DoD weapons systems.

1.1 Scope
The Contractor shall provide support to the Government in the areas ofcooperative CRA of
tactical fighter/attack and surveillance aircraft or similarly complex aircraft, smart (GPS guided)
weapons or similarly complex weapons, test equipment, software, associated communications
and networks in accordance with NAVAIR CYBERSAFE Standard Work Package (SWP) and
NAVAIR CRA SWP. Cyber Table Top (CTT) efforts will be conducted in accordance with the
NAVAIR CTT SWP.

The Contractor shall provide input for the development of a Verification and Validation (V&V)
test plan and participate in the Government led testing of tactical fighter/attack and surveillance
aircraft or similarly complex aircraft, smart (GPS guided) weapons or similarly complex
weapons, test equipment, software, associated communications and networks.

In general, the scope of this SOW includes providing cyber resources and expertise to conduct
CRAs on platform and weapon systems by interviewing system Subject Matter Experts (SMEs),
analyzing design and test documentation and operational requirements, conducting cyber
analyses and assessments and developing work products as outlined in the NAVAIR
CYBERSAFE SWP, NAVAIR CRA SWP , and NAVAIR CTT SWP to strengthen resiliency
and integrity.

3.0 Requirements
The Contractor shall provide support to the Government in the areas of Cyber vulnerability
analysis and risk assessment of NAVAIR tactical fighter/attack and surveillance aircraft or
similarly complex aircraft, smart (GPS guided) weapons or similarly complex weapons, test
equipment, software, associated communications and networks in accordance with NAVAIR
CYBERSAFE Standard Work Package (SWP), NAVAIR CRA SWP and NAVAIR CTT SWP.
The Contractor shall support the development of the V&V test plan and participate in the
Government led test. All documentation developed as part of the final Government reports will
be classified and marked in accordance with the NAVAIR Security Classification Guide (SCG)
or in the instance where a program SCG exists, the program SCG will take precedence over the
NAVAIR SCG.

The Contractor shall support the following Government led tasking related to all CRAs:
    Technical analysis
    Mission analysis
    Verification and Validation (V&V) testing

The Contractor shall support test plan development and participate in V&V testing conducted by
the Government.

A draft list and schedule of NAVAIR CRAs is found in the NAVAIR Prioritized System List.
CRAs are composed of CYBERSAFE Level(s) (CSL) 1-4 as defined in the NAVAIR
CYBERSAFE SWP.

CRAs typically consist of data/document and intelligence gathering, technical system analysis,
mission analysis and V&V

3.1 Tactical Fighter/Attack/Surveillance Platform Assessment
The Contractor shall provide cyber susceptibility/vulnerability analysis and risk assessment of
NAVAIR tactical fighter/attack and surveillance aircraft.

3.1.1 Cyber Table Top (CTT) Assessment


                                                                                         Page 2 of 6
The Contractor shall participate in a Government-led team, and shall provide written input for
CTT work products including access point maps, workbooks, risk analysis, and final assessment
report for NAVAIR tactical fighter/attack and surveillance aircraft. The Contractor shall submit
the CTT work products to the designated Government representative in accordance with the
appropriate SCG. Once work products have been submitted, the Contractor shall send a
transmittal letter to the COR.

3.1.2 Cyber Risk Assessment (CRA)
The Contractor shall participate in a Government-led team, and shall provide written input for
CRA work products including access point maps, workbooks, risk analysis, and final assessment
report for NAVAIR tactical fighter/attack and surveillance aircraft. The Contractor shall submit
the CRA work products to the designated Government representative in accordance with the
appropriate SCG. Once work products have been submitted, the Contractor shall send a
transmittal letter to the COR.

3.1.3 Verification and Validation (V&V) Testing
The Contractor shall provide input for the development of V&V test plan and observe the
Government-led test of NAVAIR tactical fighter/attack and surveillance aircraft. The Contractor
shall submit the V&V test plan inputs to the designated Government representative in
accordance with the appropriate SCG. Once test plan inputs have been submitted, the Contractor
shall send a transmittal letter to the COR.

3.1.4 Vulnerability Assessment Report (VAR)
The Contractor shall participate in a Government-led team, and shall provide written input for
VAR work products including CRA and V&V final reports for NAVAIR tactical fighter/attack
and surveillance aircraft. The Contractor shall submit the VAR work products to the designated
Government representative in accordance with the appropriate SCG. Once work products have
been submitted, the Contractor shall send a transmittal letter to the COR.

products to the designated Government representative in accordance with the appropriate SCG.

3.3 Progress Reports
The Contractor shall provide a monthly Contractor’s Progress, Status, and Management Report
that shows planned expenditures over the life of the contract and each TO, comparing planned to
actual expenditures. All data in the Contractor’s Progress, Status, and Management Report shall
be unclassified. Technical tasking progress shall be summarized to include accomplishments;
compliance to schedule; issues, problems, and solutions; and risks to completion of the TO
effort. The Contractor shall provide a summary of prime and subcontractor labor hours expended
by each TO during the reporting period and the cumulative hours expended for each TO during
the period of performance. The Travel Section of the report shall contain the following for each
travel trip taken during the reporting period: (1) Name(s) of travelers, (2) Travel dates, (3)
Destination, (4) Purpose of trip, and (5) Accomplishments during trip. (Contract Data
Requirements List (CDRL) A001)

The Contractor shall perform administrative support for the technical tasking in Paragraphs 3.1 -
3.4 to meet NAWCWD mission requirements. The Contractor shall use Government-furnished

personnel and visitor badging; coordinate and schedule team meetings and CRA interviews;
coordinate and schedule telecons and VTCs; complete, submit and track NMCI and
Communication Service Requests for team members; update and post project plans, update and
post financial reports, and progress reports to Government-owned share drive; coordinate
lab/facility access; and track and update team action items.

5.0 Special Provisions
This section describes the security, travel, and materials for this effort. The following sub-
sections provide details of various considerations on this effort.

5.1 Security
The Contractor shall provide personnel with the appropriate security clearance levels for the
work to be performed. Access to Top Secret (TS) Sensitive Compartmented Information (SCI)
information will be required in the performance of this task and shall be in accordance with
National Industrial Security Program Operating Manual (NISPOM), DoD 5220.22-M, dated
January 1995. The Contractor shall provide for the security of the information, hardware, and
software in accordance with the NISPOM. Technical documentation shall be marked with the
appropriate classification levels and shall include a distribution statement as defined in
Distribution Statements on Technical Documents, DoD D-5230.24, dated 23 August 2012. The
Contractor shall employ Operational Security (OPSEC) measures to control unclassified
information that could be considered mission/program sensitive in accordance with the Contract
DD Form 254.

5.1.1 Clearance
Certain tasks will require Contractors have a TOP SECRET/SCI. Access to non-SCI intelligence
information is required to access intelligence information to perform analyses. Access and use of
Secure Telephone Equipment is required for follow-up questions to site personnel and for
collaboration with Government subject matter experts. Access to Joint Worldwide Intelligence
Communication System (JWICS) and Secret Internet Protocol Router Network (SIPRNET) is
necessary to complete analyses and deliverables. Access to classified spaces and generation of
classified material shall be in accordance with DD Form 254. The Contractor shall obtain facility
and personnel security clearances as required by the Department of Industrial Security Program
prior to starting to work on tasks requiring clearances.

5.1.2 Physical Security
The Contractor will be provided access to designated classified areas for execution of tasking
and be responsible for safeguarding all Government information or property provided for
Contractor use. Government information, facilities, equipment and materials shall be secured as
specified by the NISPOM and the NAWCWD Command Security Manual.
